# Long-form YouTube playbook (6-10 min talking head, researched 2026-11-04)

## Benchmarks
- 5-10 min videos average ~31% viewed [vendor, Retention Rabbit]; vidIQ: ~30% realistic for
  6-9 min talking heads. MrBeast guide: losing 35% in the first minute counts as "reasonably good".
- Read the retention graph: share at 30 s ("Intro"), dips (leaving/skipping), spikes (rewatch),
  vs the grey "typical" band. Below 50% at 10-15 s = hook failed; > 70% at 30 s is solid [vendor].

## First 30 seconds (Colin & Samir)
- 0-7 s: **confirm the click**: show what the thumbnail promised (the rejection, the number).
- 7-17 s: why this is happening to *you* (the stakes).
- 15-30 s: open loop + the "A to Z" so viewers know where it's going.
  "Welcome to my first YouTube video" joke comes after it — keep it short.)

## Mid-video
- Re-hook at ~3:00 and ~6:00 (MrBeast guide): a new problem, stake or number reveal.
  "Not pace of editing, pace of new problems" (Samir).
- A visual change every 15-30 s (punch-in, screen recording, a backoffice number); B-roll only
  when it shows the thing being said. Weaker material goes in the back half.
- Chapters can lower view duration on story videos (people skip) [U]; useful for tutorials.
  For build-in-public, consider chapters only after the payoff, or none.

## Ending
- Pay off the opening promise explicitly. **Don't signal the end** ("that's it", "thank you"):
  end abruptly into a one-line tease of the next episode, over the end screen (last 5-20 s,
  1-2 elements: best next video + subscribe). MrBeast guide: "endings must always be abrupt".

## After publishing
- Check intro % at 30 s and each dip vs typical; CTR in the first 48 h; swap packaging in week 1
  if it underperforms; fix the pattern in the next video. Log it in the video's note.
